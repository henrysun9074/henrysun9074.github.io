teaching

Biology is a wonderful discipline that helps define our understanding of ourselves and our relationship to the world around us. Through teaching, I seek both to help students acquire deeper understandings of biological processes, and to impart confidence using data science and computational schools to analyze biological data. I am pursuing a Certificate in College Teaching through Duke’s Graduate School alongside accruing teaching experience primarily through serving as a guest lecturer. Below is part of a draft teaching statement I made for CCT, alongside some resources and teaching materials I’ve contributed to.

Philosophy

I aim to help students develop the ability to think like biologists, to ask meaningful questions, evaluate evidence, and connect processes across levels of biological organization. I want students to leave my classes with both a conceptual framework for understanding living systems and the quantitative and analytical skills to explore biological questions for themselves.

In particular, I emphasize data interpretation, hypothesis testing, and science communication so that students understand not just what we know, but how we know it. I aim for students to appreciate the incredible diversity and adaptability of life, and the evolutionary and ecological processes that shape that diversity. I train students to develop both conceptual understanding of and practical fluency in using computational tools to address biological questions. They learn to frame biological problems in ways that can be addressed with data-driven approaches.

I design class activities and assignments that engage students directly in the process of doing biology. My aim is for students to practice the same habits that scientists use when exploring the natural world. To do this, I incorporate data-centered activities that ask students to interpret figures from published papers, analyze real datasets using code, or draw conclusions based on evidence from experiments. I also emphasize collaborative and project-based learning, where students work together to design and carry out small investigations or analyses. Finally, I integrate opportunities for reflection and iteration, such as peer feedback, revision of written work, and discussions about uncertainty and limitations in data. These activities reinforce that science is an ongoing, evidence-based process rather than a collection of facts to memorize.

Equally important to me as any quantitative teaching approach is fostering an inclusive and supportive learning environment. I believe that, like any healthy ecosystem, a classroom thrives on diversity. Diverse perspectives and experiences strengthen scientific inquiry by shaping the questions we ask and the ways we interpret data. I strive to make all students feel seen and valued through open communication, accessible mentorship, and structured opportunities for feedback.

Outreach

Growing Equity in Science and Technology (GEST):

  • GEST is an annual outreach event hosted at the Duke University Marine Lab geared towards providing middle school students with STEM activities, knowledge, and access to role models (faculty, staff, etc.). I serve on the planning committee for GEST, help to maintain the website, and lead the creation of the GEST newsletter.

SciREN Coast:

  • Held annually in coastal North Carolina, SciREN Coast connects researchers with local grade school teachers to help develop curricula and classroom activities. In 2025, our booth, which discussed the use of technology, remote sensing, and AI in studying marine ecosystems, won runner-up! In 2026, our booth focused on showcasing biodiversity of local marine invertebrate species, and again won runner-up!
